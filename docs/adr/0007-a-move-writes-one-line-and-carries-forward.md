# A move writes one line, and carries forward from the current period

A line is moved by long-pressing its title and dragging it; there is no grip handle and no star (#29, #86). A section reads in `sortOrder` alone, so a move is a write to `sortOrder`, and `moveEntry` writes it on the moved line only: its place becomes a number between its new neighbors', one less than the first at the top, one more than the last at the bottom. Templates materialize only into the current period (ADR 0001), so nothing else would take an order to the next page; a move on the period containing today therefore carries forward, placing the line's template the same way between the templates of the recurring lines now around it. A move on a past page stays on that page: its lines are not the ones the next period is written from. A retired template is never written, and never marks a place.

Rejected: renumbering the section 1… on every move, as #29 first specified. Under the merge (ADR 0005) each renumbered line and template is a whole write, so a move would stamp lines nobody touched. A device that had not yet heard a template was retired would, by moving some other line past its line, un-retire the template and keep the line the ghost rule drops; and a check tapped on a passed line on another device would lose to the move. One write keeps a move as merge-safe as a check tap: only the moved line can lose or win.

Places are therefore not whole numbers and a page's places are not its templates'. Renumbering survives as the fallback for the cases with no room between the neighbors: two that share a place (two devices each wrote a new line offline), a gap halved until the numbers run out, or a page whose order no longer follows its templates' (retiring the star renumbered pages, not templates). Then every line, or every template on the page, is renumbered 1…. Templates not on the page, a daily one whose weekdays skip today, keep their place and can tie with one placed by a move; ties read in stored order.

The gesture never reorders anything while a finger is down. The lifted row leaves the flow and follows the finger; the others keep their order and open its slot with a margin, sliding by a layout transition; the store moves only once the drop has settled, in one commit that also remounts the dropped row. Reordering the views mid-drag would take the touch from the row being dragged, and a drag offset held on the UI thread cannot be cleared in the same frame as a React commit, so the offset leaves with the old view instead. Rows are measured, never assumed to share a height. VoiceOver and keyboard users move a line with the title's Move up and Move down actions, and on web with Alt+↑ and Alt+↓; a touch on the web build scrolls the page and does not lift.
